## Problem Statement

Small teams swing between standing broad network reach and blunt access cuts after security trouble. Broad reach lets fast intrusions roam. Blunt cuts remove the read access people need during incidents. Intra explores a private network layer where services stay unreachable by default and approved people, devices, and robots still get scoped work done through checked gateways.

- Narrowest wedge candidate inside the chosen platform: timed, read-only, logged prod database access from one approved Mac during an incident, with no broad VPN reach. Read-only is enforced by the database, not by the proxy: the broker issues a Postgres role with `SELECT`-only grants, `default_transaction_read_only` on, and a `VALID UNTIL` expiry, and no write ever uses a broader role. The grant secret stays in broker memory only (eng-review R1). The Mac or robot authenticates to the broker with its mesh certificate plus a grant token and never receives the Postgres password. One secret exists per grant. The broker wipes it at grant end. `VALID UNTIL` alone does not end live access, so at grant expiry or revocation the broker terminates every session using the issued role, then disables and drops the role. Maximum session lifetime equals the grant TTL. Pooled connections bind to one grant and close with it. No pooled session survives into a later grant. In the lab, one robot acts only as a test actor that proves denial and grant mechanics. Robots become a committed product actor only after the demand gate, and then use the same grant flow with no self-approval.

## Constraints

- First build target is a personal lab on one Mac. Fake services and fake secrets only.
- Compose existing mesh and proxy parts. Do not build crypto, mesh, or TLS code.
- Deterministic rules decide access. No LLM judge in the allow path. One policy source feeds generated mesh, guard, and broker configs (CEO review D16). Generated diffs are reviewable and testable. Hand edits to generated configs are forbidden.
- Security claim is containment and smaller blast radius, never attack prevention.
- Identity plane matters as much as network route. A private route alone does not stop abuse through trusted vendor or SaaS access.
- Non-goal for Stages 0-5: trusted-vendor and SaaS identity abuse that never traverses the private network. The committed stages claim nothing there. It needs its own later design.

## Threat Model

- Attacker A, unknown device: no mesh identity. Gates: no route to any service, guard rejects.
- Attacker B, compromised lab robot: valid robot identity, hostile intent. Gates: normal-service access only, sensitive service denied, direct routes fail, grant requests cannot self-approve.
- Attacker C, compromised normal service: tries lateral reach. Gates: no route to the sensitive service or guard admin path.
- The fake attacker server is a passive sink. A canary leak means `CANARY-SECRET-123` reaches that server, any unauthorized actor, or the log and replay corpora. An authorized Mac read of the fake secret is expected lab exposure, not a leak.
- Non-claims: an authorized reader copying data out by hand, and malware fully controlling the approved Mac during a live grant. Short grants and step-up checks reduce that window later. They do not remove it.
- Broker compromise: the broker is a high-value target. Its database credential lives in an owner-only (`0600`) file or OS keychain entry readable only by the broker account. That database role may create, alter, and drop read-only grant roles and terminate their sessions. It holds no `SELECT` grant on protected data. Only the guard may call the broker, through a local socket with mutual certificate auth. No broker admin path exists on the network. Broker issuance needs an owner approval record signed by the bound Mac key. Residual risk stays named: a fully compromised broker can deny service and attempt grant abuse within its database rights. Stage 3 cannot enter until tests prove the broker credential cannot read protected data and unsigned issuance fails.
- Grant TTL caps: human sensitive-service grants max 8 hours, human normal-service grants max 24 hours, incident bundles max 8 hours, robot grants max 2 hours inside a template and 8 hours with human approval. Renewal always needs fresh approval. It never extends an old grant silently.
- Sensitive service rule: the service owner classifies at registration and records the reason. A service is sensitive if it holds secrets, personal, customer, or payment data, production data, admin or control functions, or can issue credentials. When unsure, classify sensitive.
- Approver recovery: a new approver device binds only from local console on the guard host, with the old device revoked first or declared lost in the emergency log. Stage 5 entry needs one tested backup and restore of the grant store and audit log to a clean host.

## Enforcement Topology

- The sensitive service (lab: Postgres) binds only to a guard-only network. Postgres accepts connections only from the broker database gateway identity (CEO review D12). HTTP services accept connections only from the guard identity. Neither has a public port or a direct mesh route from the Mac or the robot.
- Mesh rules allow Mac-to-guard and robot-to-guard only. Firewall default is deny on every lab network.
- Lab containers use one internal resolver. Direct external DNS and direct internet egress from lab networks are denied, so DNS tricks have no path out.
- Lab gate mapping: direct IP and alternate ports hit no listener outside the guard path. No-mesh clients have no route. DNS-named canary queries die at the resolver and egress deny.

## Identity Lifecycle

- Enrollment: the founder approves each device in the mesh by hand. The owner login binds to the Mac device key in mesh plus proxy auth. Either signal alone is not enough for sensitive access.
- Robot identity: one key per robot, issued by a founder-run lab script, never shared, never copied from the Mac key.
- Revocation: lost Mac or suspect robot means remove the device from the mesh and delete its grants. Expired keys, tokens, and grants deny by default.
- Approver auth: the owner approves from the bound Mac with an active login, Touch ID confirmation for every sensitive-service grant (CEO review D14), and an explicit confirm action. The Touch ID result gates release of the Mac approval signing key. A failed or unavailable Touch ID check denies the sensitive grant. In the one-person lab the owner may approve the owner's own fake-data grants, with reason logged. Robots never approve, including their own requests. Real-service stages revisit two-person approval.

## Guard Failure Behavior

- Fail closed. If the guard is down, sensitive and normal lab services stay unreachable over the network. There is no network fail-open path at any stage.
- The founder operates the guard host and owns restart. Stage 5 needs a tested restart and rollback runbook before entry.
- Emergency path while the guard is down: physical or local console on the guard host only. The founder records reason and time in a local append-only emergency log, merged into the audit log after recovery. Incident reads wait for guard recovery. That cost is accepted over silent open access.

## Log and Store Rules

- Logs and replay corpora hold decision metadata only: actor, device, service, action class, rule, result, grant id, timestamps. No request bodies, no query results, no query literals, no secrets, no canary. Lab retention is 30 days. Real stages set retention at Stage 5 entry, default 90 days. Only the owner reads lab logs.
- Grant files are owner-only (`0600`) and writable only by the broker process account. Audit records form a hash chain: each record hashes the prior record. A chain alone only detects partial edits, because guard-host write access could recompute it. The guard therefore copies a signed chain head daily to the founder Mac, outside the guard host. Stage 5 entry requires passing chain-verify against an external anchor and permission tests, or a store replacement that gives the same guarantees.
- Session-kill deadline: local database, log, and metric sessions must die within 60 seconds of expiry or revoke in tests. SaaS sessions must die within 15 minutes or that provider stays inventory-only.
- Audit failure rule, CEO review D13: if the audit queue cannot write, the broker issues no new grants. Existing sessions run only to their already-logged expiry. The user sees an explicit audit-paused error. No silent grant exists.

## Components and Terms

- Mesh: device identity and encrypted routes only. It never decides service actions.
- Guard: the identity-aware proxy on the guard host. It decides allow or deny per service and action class, and writes decision metadata.
- Broker: a separate process on the guard host. It owns grant requests, approval records, the Postgres data path as database gateway, Postgres role issuance, session termination, and revocation. CEO review D12 settled this transport choice, so the HTTP proxy pick does not need to speak Postgres.
- Grant store: owner-only files written only by the broker account. Audit log: hash-chained records appended by one audit queue writer only (eng-review R2). Guard and broker submit events to that queue and never write the chain file.
- "Gateway" in this document means the guard path as seen by a client. It is not a separate component.
- Incident room: one incident_id grant bundle across named services, one expiry, one revoke, one timeline.
- Task-scoped: a grant bound to one named task, service set, action class, requester identity, and expiry. It cannot be reused for another task.
- First guided safe cut: after VPN or mesh import, the system shows standing paths and converts exactly one low-risk path to guard-only, then runs allow and bypass tests.
- Standing-reach score: defined in Stage 9. Lower is better.

## Recommended Approach

Build Approach B in hard stages. No stage expands scope on a demo alone.

CrabTrap, defined once: an open-source forward proxy that sits between AI agents and external APIs, checks outbound HTTP requests against static rules and an LLM judge, and logs each decision. Intra borrows only three setup patterns from it: watch mode before enforcement, draft rules from observed traffic, and offline replay of recorded traffic against new rules. Its LLM judge and outbound scope stay out of this design.

Stages 0-2 are the allowed tiny prototype under premise 3. They may run before interviews finish. Entry to Stage 3 is blocked until the Success Criteria demand gate passes and the kill gate does not fire.

1. Stage 0, pick the parts. Entry: none. Selection criteria: device-bound identity, per-service and per-action policy, metadata log quality, revocation speed, and a written list of hosted components and trust. "No hosted control plane" bans a hosted Intra control plane only. A vendor-hosted mesh coordination server (Tailscale default) is allowed only if recorded as accepted trust. Picks recorded in the repo at `docs/STAGE0.md`: Tailscale as target mesh, local CA mTLS plus Docker networks as the lab substitute, custom Go guard, this Mac as lab guard host. Implementation language is settled as Go for broker, policy compiler, CLI, and audit queue writer (eng-review R3), with a small signed macOS helper for Touch ID. Exit gate: written picks, each scored against every criterion, plus a proof test that the broker-owned database gateway transports a Postgres read under a read-only role, a write attempt fails at the database, and expiry kills the live session inside 60 seconds.
2. Stage 1, lab skeleton. Entry: Stage 0 exit. Fake Postgres holds `CANARY-SECRET-123`. One normal fake service and one fake attacker server (passive sink from the threat model) complete the lab. Exit gate: every lab request appears in guard logs as metadata only, and a scan finds no canary in logs.
3. Stage 2, enforce the first slice. Entry: Stage 1 exit. Authorization here is one static, lab-only rule: bound Mac device plus owner login may read the fake sensitive service. That standing rule exists for fake data only, expires with the lab, and Stage 3 replaces it. No standing grant of any kind survives into Stage 5. The lab robot test actor reads only the normal service. Unknown devices and direct routes fail. Exit gate: zero canary leaks, zero open bypass paths per the topology mapping, 100 percent of decisions logged.
4. Stage 3, just-in-time grants. Entry: demand gate passed, kill gate not fired. Read access needs a reason, a short expiry, a client request key, and human approval. One request key maps to at most one grant (CEO review D15). Retries return that grant in its current state and never mint a twin role. Robots cannot approve their own grants. Exit gate: expired grants deny, unapproved requests deny, write attempts fail at the database role, a session opened before expiry cannot read after expiry or revocation, every grant and query class appears in the hash-chained audit log.
5. Stage 4, learn and replay. Entry: Stage 3 exit. Watch mode records decision metadata only. Rule drafts come from observed traffic groups. Offline replay scores each new rule set before enforcement. Robot egress control stays out of Stages 0-5 as later scope. Exit gate: clean replay as defined in Success Criteria, and no rule enforces without a replay record.
6. Stage 5, first real service. Entry gates, all required: completed security review by a reviewer who did not build the guard, covering threat model, topology, identity lifecycle, broker compromise, stores, and failure behavior, with no unresolved Critical or High finding; one named service with a named owner who consents in writing; low-risk means no customer data, no personal data, no payment data, and no prod secrets; recorded two-person approval decision (adopt it, or accept solo approval in writing for this pilot only); recorded log-retention decision; tested grant-store and audit-log backup and restore; tested approver-device re-binding; guard store chain-verify against the external anchor and permission tests pass; recorded guard-host availability decision revisiting Mac-first sleep risk (stay Mac-first with accepted downtime, or move the guard to an always-on host); tested rollback returns that one named service to watch-mode routing for at most 24 hours, owner-approved and logged, in under 15 minutes. Stage 5 is a founder-operated pilot, not a real-user release for E6. Effort estimates for Stages 0-5: 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, and 3 founder-weeks (14 total, estimated). If 12 calendar months pass before Stage 5 exit, demand evidence refreshes before any expansion entry. Rollback never restores broad standing network reach and never fires automatically to an unguarded state. Watch mode first. Enforcement needs its own clean replay on that named service: same under-1-percent metric and zero false blocks on the sensitive-read class, computed on at least 200 watch-mode decisions from that service recorded across at least 7 days before enforcement. A lab clean replay alone never authorizes real enforcement.

Egress topology: robot networks have no direct internet route and no direct external DNS; the proxy is the only exit and denies private, loopback, and metadata destinations. Pre-approved task templates may auto-issue read-only grants inside named services, action classes, and TTL bounds set by a human. Any grant outside a template, any write, and any sensitive service waits for human approval. Robots cannot create or edit templates and never self-approve. ## Success Criteria

- Demand gate: 5 practitioner interviews done, recruited per the Assignment mix. At least 3 of the 5, and at least 2 of the tech-lead or infra-owner subgroup, describe flat reach, a blunt cut, or blocked incident reads in their own words. At least 2 agree to observe a lab walkthrough or share a sanitized access map.
- Lab gate: Mac allowed, robot denied on the sensitive service, unknown device denied, zero canary leaks, zero bypass paths across direct IP, alternate ports, no-mesh, and DNS tricks.
- Log gate: every decision record names actor, device, service, action, rule, and result.
- Performance gate (CEO review D18): added p95 latency stays under 50 ms versus direct fake-service reads in the lab, measured per release on the grant read path. This is an estimated target, not a measured baseline.
- Replay gate and "clean replay" mean one defined metric: false blocks divided by total recorded normal decisions, times 100, is under 1 percent, on a corpus of at least 200 normal decisions recorded across at least 5 separate lab sessions. Zero false blocks may occur on Mac-reads-sensitive decisions. This lab corpus definition governs Stage 4 exit only. Stage 5 enforcement uses the same metric and zero-false-block class rule on the named real service corpus defined in Stage 5.
- Kill gate: if interviews rank split human and robot policy above blocked incident access, or incumbents already cover the wedge to user satisfaction, stop platform work and reframe.
